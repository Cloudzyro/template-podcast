Marisol Ventura has run the ovens at Corner Loaf for eleven years. Her day starts when most of Eastgate's ends: the night bus at 11:40, keys in the back door at midnight, and the first trays in by one.

We recorded in the bakery between the second and third bakes, sitting on flour sacks while the proofing cabinet hummed. She explains how a starter can sulk on a cold night, what she does when a 300-loaf order arrives with six hours' notice, and the small economy of the back door, where night nurses and taxi drivers trade coffee for day-old rolls.

## In this episode

- Why a bakery's busiest hour is 4 a.m.
- The three times Marisol has slept through her alarm, and what happened each time
- How to tell a loaf is done by the sound
- The first loaf rule, and who gets it instead

## A moment from the recording

> "People think the night is quiet. The night is the loudest part of my day. The mixer, the oven fans, the radio I can't turn off because the knob broke in 2019."
